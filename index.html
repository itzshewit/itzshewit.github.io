Building education systems that don't collapse when WiFi does

Computer Scince student in Addis Ababa. 2021 hit different—sitting in displacement housing doing derivatives while friends who'd crushed national math competitions watched three years of university evaporate because nobody planned for "what if everything just stops." The derivative of x² is still 2x whether you're in a climate-controlled library or using your phone's flashlight to see your notebook. That broke something in me. Or fixed it. Now I only build things that work when infrastructure fails, when power's unstable, when internet's a luxury. AI tutors that refuse to just give you the answer because dependency isn't learning. Lesson plans that go stupidly deep on three concepts instead of skimming thirty because I'm tired of watching people "complete" courses and remember nothing two weeks later. Trying to figure out what actually causes understanding to stick versus what just feels productive in the moment.

Educational Platform for Ethiopian Students

EdTech Web Development Content Design

Problem: High school students in Ethiopia lack access to structured, high-quality learning resources tailored to their curriculum and learning pace.

Solution: Designed and prototyped a learning platform focused on Grade 12 students preparing for university. The platform includes organized lesson notebooks, practice problems, and interactive explanations for subjects like mathematics and English.

Tools: Conceptual design, curriculum structuring, content creation, user experience mapping.

Status: Built. Not launched. Turns out great content without trust networks just sits there. Learning that the hard way.

What I built

Three subjects, done right. Curriculum-aligned but deeper than school requirements. Local-first (Ethiopia) instead of trying to be global from day one. Quality floor I won't compromise on.

What I refused

Gamification that rewards showing up instead of understanding. Social features that optimize for viral spread. Marketplace dynamics that push quantity. Basically everything that would've made it easier to grow but worse at actually teaching.

What I learned

Being pedagogically correct doesn't matter if nobody shows up. Students trust other students more than content creators. Built the right thing, wrong order—should've built trust infrastructure first, content second.

What I'm fixing

Ship to 10 real students first. Actually watch where they get confused instead of guessing. Build AI tutoring into the foundation. Stop building features nobody asked for.

The part that hurts: Everything I built was pedagogically sound. The sequencing works. The depth is there. Students who try it learn better. But almost nobody tries it because I forgot that adoption isn't about quality—it's about trust. And you can't build trust with a product sitting on a server. Still learning this lesson, slowly and expensively.

AI Tutoring System — Socratic Architecture

AI Ethics Pedagogy Question Design

Constraint: AI that gives answers makes students dependent. AI that asks questions can make them think.

Approach: Built a conversational system that responds to student questions with progressively guiding questions, not direct answers. The system detects conceptual gaps and constructs question sequences that lead students toward understanding through their own reasoning.

Ethical boundary: Refuses to complete homework. Refuses to give final answers. Only guides toward student-generated solutions.

How it actually works: Students get frustrated at first (they want answers, not questions). Then something shifts—they start solving things they couldn't before. Slower than just giving answers, yeah. But they remember it six months later, which matters more.

Educational Content — Conceptual Reconstruction

Explanation Cognitive Load Transfer

Design principle: Most educational content optimizes for retention. I optimize for reconstruction—can the student rebuild the concept from scratch after watching?

Method: No inspirational hooks. No narrative dramatization. Each video isolates one concept, breaks it into minimal components, then rebuilds it with the student. Mathematics, logic, AI principles, ethics—all treated as structures that can be understood through careful assembly.

Standard: If explanation requires metaphor, understanding is incomplete. Simplification is acceptable. Imprecision is not. Content ships when it meets this threshold, not when timelines suggest.

Status: Production system built. Content developing. Publishing pace determined by quality floor, not posting schedule. Deliberate over frequent.

export Content & Teaching

Teaching isn't really about explaining well—though that helps. It's building environments where struggle becomes productive instead of just frustrating. Minimal distractions, transparent structure, permission to fail without social penalty. Then mostly you just wait. Don't rescue people the second they look confused. Let the discomfort do its job. The actual learning happens in that silence after you've explained something and before they get it, not during your explanation. That's where brains rewire themselves. Your job is to not interrupt that process.

What Drives Me

Most ed-tech optimizes for vanity metrics: engagement, completion rates, minutes spent on platform. All useless. I'm building for transfer—can someone solve a problem they've never seen before, months after using your system? Completely different goal, completely different architecture. Grows slower, teaches better. Harder to get funding for, easier to defend.

The question with AI in education isn't "can it scale access" (obviously yes, that's solved). It's whether we can build systems that preserve what makes human teaching actually work: diagnosing exactly where someone's mental model breaks, asking the specific question that unsticks them, holding space while their brain does the messy work of reorganizing around a new concept. Technology should amplify that diagnostic precision, not try to automate it away with generic responses.

Long-term bet: infrastructure over individual products. Systems other educators can fork and modify for their context. Where structure enables exploration instead of constraining it. Where high standards open doors because expectations are transparent from day one, not closed because they're arbitrary gatekeeping. This takes years to build correctly. Most funding models optimize for quarters not years. Still trying to figure out how to make that math work without compromising on what matters.
